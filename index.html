PRJ.01 — LEAD PROJECT

VTOL Quadplane

Avionics Flight Systems Lead · Third-Year Group Project, University of Sheffield

Tasked with designing and manufacturing a Vertical Take-Off and Landing (VTOL) Quadplane intended for reconnaissance in hazardous environments. As Avionics Flight Systems Lead, responsible for the electronics architecture, wiring, and flight testing — the aircraft flew successfully at a local gliding club under both manual and autonomous control.

What

  • Designed and manufactured a VTOL Quadplane for reconnaissance in hazardous environments
  • Flew the aircraft at a local gliding club under both manual and autonomous control
  • Produced a technical report and poster presentation on the design process and flight performance

How

  • Analysed mission specifications and developed a working design plan
  • Iterated the airframe as a digital twin in Fusion 360
  • Manufactured the frame using 3D printing and basic workshop tools
  • Wired and soldered all avionics to fit inside the fuselage
  • Collaborated with the team to write the technical report

Results

  • Successful flight showing impressive stability across flight modes
  • Autonomous flight achieved, including hover, loiter and transition
  • Full recovery of flight telemetry, enabling in-depth analysis of flight path, battery health and stability

Project HEX

Manufacturing Team · Extracurricular Group Project, University of Sheffield
PRJ.02

Design and manufacture of a 3m wingspan VTOL drone, built by a team of 50 students to compete in the USA's SUAS (Student Unmanned Aerial Systems) competition. Worked within the manufacturing team producing carbon fibre wing and fuselage components.

What

  • Designed and manufactured a 3m wingspan VTOL drone for the SUAS competition in the USA
  • Designed and produced moulds for the wing and fuselage

How

  • Created toolpaths in Fusion 360
  • Operated a CNC router to manufacture carbon fibre moulds
  • Carried out wet and dry carbon fibre layup

Results

  • Fully functional VTOL drone with successful vertical take-off and transition to forward flight
Outcome — An unforeseen mechanical failure in the aileron caused the drone to crash during final testing, and it did not make the competition. The airframe itself performed as designed up to that point.
